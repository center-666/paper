1.  Introduction

    Over the past decade, research on cyber-physical systems (CPSs) has received increasing attention [1–3]. These systems are becom-
ing more common in all areas of modern society, including smart grids, robotics, and intelligent transportation systems. However, due
to the open connectivity of communication networks, CPSs are often vulnerable to cyber-attacks [4–6]. Since high-precision feedback
control fundamentally hinges on the accuracy of sensed information, the integrity of measurement data and the subsequent state
estimation process essentially dictate the overall system reliability. Once the estimation accuracy is compromised, the operation of
CPSs can be severely disrupted by such malicious attacks. This motivates the study of secure state estimation against cyber-attacks.
    Among various cyber-attacks, denial-of-service (DoS) attacks and false data injection (FDI) attacks are two widely studied classes.
The former blocks information exchange, aiming to exhaust the communication resources of the system [7,8]. In response to DoS
attacks and to ensure the normal operation of the system, various secure control and scheduling methods were developed [9–11].
Diﬀerent from DoS attacks, FDI attacks can compromise the integrity of the data and then degrade the accuracy of state estimation
results [12–14]. Moreover, by employing meticulously designed attack vectors, FDI attacks can inject erroneous data that circumvents
bad data detection mechanisms. This makes the design of secure state estimation schemes against FDI attacks particularly crucial.
    The existing research on secure state estimation against FDI attacks adopts two perspectives: attacker and defender. From the
perspective of the attacker, various problems have been investigated, such as identifying vulnerabilities and attack design [15,16].
From the perspective of the defender, some problems, such as resilience estimation and attack detection, have been studied [17].

However, these results primarily focus on centralized frameworks, which may become computationally ineﬃcient and less scalable
as the system scale grows and the sensor population increases [18,19]. Speciﬁcally, the centralized processing center often faces a
communication and computation bottleneck, necessitating the transition to distributed estimation. In distributed cases, sensors can
only rely on local information from neighboring nodes. Furthermore, limited computational and communication resources coupled
with sparse communication links make secure estimation strategies under distributed frameworks more challenging.
    In the ﬁeld of distributed secure state estimation against FDI attacks, conventional research often prioritizes resilient estimation
[20]. These schemes typically use robust gain design or nonlinear weighting strategies to suppress the inﬂuence of malicious data
and keep estimation errors within a controllable range by limiting the impact of compromised information on the fusion process.
However, such passive resilience often comes at the cost of estimation precision, driving the need for active detection-based strategies
that can identify and isolate malicious data. To enhance the resilience of consensus-based distributed estimation against FDI attacks, a
localized protection mechanism leveraging online innovation sequences was introduced in [21]. However, an attacker can circumvent
this defense by manipulating the estimate alone while keeping the transmitted innovation unchanged. To counter stealthy attacks
that independently manipulate estimates and innovations, [22] introduces a novel 𝜒2-like detection mechanism designed to monitor
both signals simultaneously. Notably, their scheme requires the dual transmission of innovations for detection and local estimates for
consensus-based fusion. However, the dual transmission of these signals increases the communication burden and complicates the
fusion process in heterogeneous networks, posing scalability challenges for large-scale applications. An alternative strategy involves
constructing a local residual using the received estimates and performing FDI attack detection, thereby mitigating the vulnerabilities
associated with transmitting both residuals and estimates [23,24]. However, the authors in [25] revealed that an attacker can bypass
the detector and degrade state estimation performance when using the aforementioned local residual construction method. Speciﬁ-
cally, this vulnerability emerges for a rank-deﬁcient measurement matrix, i.e., when the measurement dimension is smaller than the
state dimension. In practical distributed architectures, a rank-deﬁcient measurement matrix is common due to sensors not being able
to always capture the full state vector [26]. In light of the unresolved security vulnerabilities, the conventional approach of trans-
mitting state estimates in existing methods has been reassessed. Since residuals are directly related to measurement inconsistencies,
they are more suitable for integrity veriﬁcation than state estimates.
    This paper proposes a distributed secure estimation scheme against FDI attacks by exchanging residuals instead of estimates. Since
neighboring sensors may generate dimension-mismatched residuals [27], a regularization mapping is introduced for the fusion. The
contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

1.  In contrast to the existing estimates-exchanging distributed secure estimation scheme [21–24], a residual-exchanging scheme is
    introduced. By deploying standard 𝜒2 detectors to verify the integrity of these residuals directly, the proposed scheme signiﬁcantly
    reduces communication bandwidth requirements.
2.  A distributed estimator is proposed that integrates the regularized mapping mechanism to fuse the residuals received from neigh-
    boring sensor nodes. It is proved that the estimation error is mean-square bounded under a closed-loop Lyapunov stability condi-
    tion.
3.  For attackers without real-time residual access, it is shown that any nonzero independent bias increases the expected detection
    statistic. For attackers with real-time residual access, a dynamic watermarking mechanism is introduced to reveal a representative
    reﬂection-type stealthy attack.

    The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the system model and formulates the problem. In Section 3,
a distributed estimator is proposed, and the boundedness of the state estimation error is analyzed. Section 4 investigates the security
of the proposed distributed estimator. An illustrative example based on a constant velocity model is presented in Section 5, and
Section 6 concludes the paper.

2.2.  Distributed estimator model

    The residual represents the diﬀerence between the actual measurements and the predictions based on the model, and under normal
conditions, it usually appears as white noise with a zero mean [5]. However, when faults or attacks occur, this residual undergoes
noticeable changes in its statistical behavior, which is then used as a key indicator for triggering alarms.

        𝑧𝑖,𝑘 ≜ 𝑦𝑖,𝑘 − 𝐻𝑖 ̂𝑥𝑖,𝑘,                                                                                                                                 (2)

2.4.  Problem of interest

    This paper aims to develop a secure distributed estimation scheme for CPSs against FDI attacks. Speciﬁcally, attack detection is
performed by leveraging the statistical properties of the exchanged residuals, followed by the use of authenticated residuals for state
estimation.
    The problems addressed in this paper are formulated as follows.

1.  Develop a distributed estimator that fuses local residuals and veriﬁed neighboring residuals, and derive the optimal gain 𝐾⋆𝑖,𝑘 by
    minimizing the trace of the local estimation error covariance.
2.  Analyze the security performance of the proposed scheme and analyze the expected detection statistic under two attacker knowl-
    edge patterns.

        𝔼[ ̃𝐽 𝑎𝑖𝑗,𝑘] = 𝔼[𝐽 0𝑖𝑗,𝑘] + 4𝐿 Tr(Ω−1𝑗  Σ𝜉,𝑗),
which proves (40). Since Ω−1𝑗   ≻ 0 and Σ𝜉,𝑗 ⪰ 0, Σ𝜉,𝑗 ≠ 0, the trace term is strictly positive, and the attack is detectable in expectation.
Remark 3.  The security assumption behind the watermarking mechanism is that the seed used to generate 𝜉𝑗,𝑘 is not available to the
attacker. Under perfect seed synchronization, the watermark is removed before both detection and fusion, so the nominal estimator
dynamics are unchanged. Moreover, the expected detection margin grows linearly with the window length 𝐿 and with Tr(Ω−1𝑗  Σ𝜉,𝑗).
When 𝐿 = 1, (40) reduces to 𝔼[ ̃𝐽 𝑎𝑖𝑗,𝑘] − 𝔼[𝐽 0𝑖𝑗,𝑘] = 4 Tr(Ω−1𝑗  Σ𝜉,𝑗).

6.  Conclusion

    This paper studies the problem of distributed secure state estimation for cyber-physical systems under false data injection attacks.
A distributed estimation scheme is developed in which sensor nodes exchange local residuals to enable both attack detection and
estimation updates. To handle dimensional mismatches among residuals from heterogeneous sensors, a regularization mapping is
applied. The optimal estimator gain is derived by minimizing the trace of the estimation error covariance. Furthermore, it is shown
that the estimation error is mean-square bounded under a closed-loop Lyapunov stability condition. The integrated 𝜒2 detector
identiﬁes anomalies for attackers without real-time residual access. For attackers with real-time access, a dynamic watermarking
